How Small Gaps Lift Water

physics experimentGuide: Julius Sumner Miller · historical science educator

Compare how far water travels between nearby surfaces, and what happens when the space narrows.

What it demonstrates

Capillary rise is stronger in narrower wettable gaps because the interaction of surface tension and adhesion with geometry can counteract gravity.

Open experiment instructions

Materials

  • Two clear plastic ruler-like strips
  • Two paper clips
  • A rubber band
  • Shallow tray
  • Tinted water

Procedure

  1. Place spacers between the strips and hold them together loosely with a rubber band.
  2. Dip the bottom edge into a shallow tray of colored water.
  3. Observe the water level.
  4. Move one spacer away to make a tapered gap, then compare water levels in wide and narrow regions.

Safety: Use plastic strips rather than glass to avoid cuts; clean up spills.
